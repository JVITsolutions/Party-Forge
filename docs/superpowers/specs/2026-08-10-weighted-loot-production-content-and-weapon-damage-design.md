# Weighted Loot Production Content and Weapon Damage Design

Date: 2026-08-10

Status: Approved design for weighted-loot Increment 3

Parent design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-08-weighted-loot-generation-and-equipment-stats-design.md`

This document is authoritative where it is more specific than the parent design. It extends Increment 3 with immutable, typed weapon base-damage ranges that are separate from implicits and explicit affixes.

## Goal

Turn the deterministic weighted-loot and equipment-stat foundations into an initial production content set that can support broad build variety, meaningful rarity and item-level progression, explainable balancing, and future base-item progression.

Increment 3 authors the first production affix and implicit libraries, adds immutable weapon base damage, upgrades item serialization explicitly, and produces deterministic balance evidence. It does not connect enemy ground drops or add the Developer Loot Lab interface; those remain Increment 4.

## Approved decisions

- Author exactly 96 production explicit affixes: 64 focused, 24 standard hybrids, and 8 premium hybrids.
- Split the explicit pool evenly into 48 prefixes and 48 suffixes.
- Give every one of the 99 active equipment bases a distinct implicit ID and display name.
- Build the 99 implicits from shared, validated balance templates rather than 99 unrelated formulas.
- Give every production explicit affix and implicit twelve ascending tiers.
- Preserve existing item and affix IDs. The six explicit fixtures count within the 96; `tempered_edge` counts within the 99 implicits.
- Constrain combat gear by archetype, slot, item type, and damage-type tags while allowing rings, amulets, and belts to act as broad build enablers.
- Use a semantic prefix/suffix split instead of mirrored or slot-dependent duplicates.
- Use progressive rarity ceilings of T3, T5, T8, T10, and T12 from Common through Legendary.
- Use the approved nonlinear item-level thresholds from 1 through 1000.
- Use relative affix-weight bands of 1000, 500, 150, and 25.
- Store weapon base-damage ranges separately from implicits and explicit affixes.
- Roll weapon damage once, store the exact typed components on the item, and never recalculate an issued weapon from live balance data.
- Scale the eligible weapon range by item level and by rarity multipliers of 1.00, 1.08, 1.18, 1.32, and 1.50.
- Let actions opt into weapon damage with an authored effectiveness multiplier while retaining authored fallback damage.
- Keep the Character Ledger Equipment & Inventory page as a separate planned UI increment.

## Scope

Included:

- 96 production explicit-affix resources and their explicit catalog registration;
- 99 base-specific implicit resources and one implicit assignment per active base;
- twelve tiers per explicit affix and implicit;
- production modifier families, weights, tags, restrictions, names, roll bounds, and advanced-tooltip metadata;
- deterministic content authoring and byte-parity generation tools;

- weapon-aware runtime damage and ledger/comparison estimates;

Deferred:

- the Developer Loot Lab screen and its controller/mouse interface;

- triggered, conditional, ailment, summon, skill-transforming, and rule-breaking affixes;
- Legendary special powers and Mythic-through-Eternal acquisition systems;
- additional equipment bases and final mode-specific item-level/drop-rate balance;

## Production content architecture

### Deterministic authoring source

Reviewable typed source rows are the authoring source of truth. A deterministic Godot builder emits categorized `.tres` resources, base assignments, weapon profiles, and the explicit foundation-catalog registration.

The source is separated by purpose:

- focused attributes and primary power;
- physical, elemental, archetype, and general offense;
- health, armor, avoidance, block, regeneration, leech, and resistances;
- cadence, geometry, movement, pickup, and support utility;
- standard two-effect hybrids;
- premium two-effect hybrids;
- base-specific implicits grouped by equipment family;
- typed weapon-damage profiles grouped by weapon family.

The builder must be deterministic under a cold import. A second run against unchanged source rows must change zero generated bytes. Hand-editing generated production resources is unsupported; changes belong in the source rows and are regenerated.

### Explicit content totals

The production catalog contains exactly:

| Content class | Prefixes | Suffixes | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Focused | 32 | 32 | 64 |
| Standard hybrid | 12 | 12 | 24 |
| Premium hybrid | 4 | 4 | 8 |
| **Total** | **48** | **48** | **96** |

The existing `stout`, `keen`, `wise`, `of_embers`, `of_rime`, and `of_reach` IDs are promoted into these totals. Their issued identities remain valid. Production values and tiers may be expanded through the same definitions without renaming the IDs.

### Base implicits

Every active equipment base references exactly one registered implicit definition. The catalog therefore contains exactly 99 production implicits.

Each implicit has:

- a stable base-specific ID;
- a player-facing name;
- one or two numerical effects;
- one of the shared slot/archetype balance templates;
- twelve tiers;
- explicit generation, rarity, source, family, and tag validation.

`tempered_edge` remains assigned to `forge_vanguard_sword` and counts as that base's unique implicit. Other bases receive new stable IDs even when they share the same template and numerical curve.

Implicits are guaranteed base properties but remain immutable `ItemAffixInstance` records after issuance. They do not consume prefix or suffix capacity.

## Eligibility and affix grammar

### Combat gear

Weapons, shields, quivers, focuses, tomes, armor, and other combat gear filter affixes through normalized base tags. Eligibility may require or exclude:

- melee, ranged, or caster archetypes;
- weapon, armor, accessory, or support roles;
- exact item types or weapon families;
- compatible action tags;
- physical, fire, cold, lightning, chaos, or future damage types;
- source and generation domain;
- rarity and item-level gates;
- progression unlock tags;
- modifier-family conflicts.

Filtering always happens before weighting. A positive weight cannot restore an ineligible affix or tier.

### Accessories

Rings, amulets, and belts use the broadest valid pool. They may roll attributes, defenses, resistances, elements, critical stats, utility, and compatible hybrids regardless of the original class presentation family.

The base's melee/ranged/caster family influences relative selection weight but does not hard-lock the accessory to one class. Global and off-archetype outcomes remain reachable.

### Semantic prefix and suffix roles

Prefixes primarily carry:

- general, archetype, physical, and elemental damage;
- maximum health and armor;
- healing power and other primary support power;
- build-defining offensive or defensive magnitude.

Suffixes primarily carry:

- Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma;
- fire, cold, lightning, and chaos resistance;
- critical chance and multiplier;
- attack speed and cooldown recovery;
- range, projectile speed, and area size;
- movement, pickup, avoidance, block, recovery, and utility.

A hybrid occupies the side matching its dominant effect. Modifier families prevent a focused affix and an equivalent hybrid component from stacking when the combination is declared exclusive.

## Weights and hybrid power

Explicit affix weights use four relative bands:

| Band | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Core focused | 1000 |
| Specialized focused | 500 |
| Standard hybrid | 150 |
| Premium hybrid | 25 |

High item level and diminishing Charisma may improve the relative odds of scarce affixes through the existing bounded policy. They cannot authorize a locked affix, defeat tag/family restrictions, exceed a tier roll range, or make the premium band common.

Standard hybrids default to 70% of the corresponding focused value for each component. Premium hybrids default to 85%. Values use stat-appropriate rounding, probability caps, and display precision. An exception must be explicit in the source row, remain monotonic across all twelve tiers, and appear in the generated balance report.

## Tier model

Tier numbers ascend in power: T1 is the weakest and T12 is the strongest initial tier.

### Item-level thresholds

| Tier | Minimum item level |
|---|---:|
| T1 | 1 |
| T2 | 10 |
| T3 | 30 |
| T4 | 60 |
| T5 | 100 |
| T6 | 160 |
| T7 | 240 |
| T8 | 340 |
| T9 | 460 |
| T10 | 600 |
| T11 | 770 |
| T12 | 950 |

All 96 explicit affixes and all 99 implicits use this shared threshold table in Increment 3. The source model may support stricter per-definition thresholds in a later increment, but no production definition authored here overrides this table.

### Rarity ceilings

| Ordinary rarity | Maximum eligible tier |
|---|---:|
| Common | T3 |
| Uncommon | T5 |
| Rare | T8 |
| Epic | T10 |
| Legendary | T12 |

Rarity supplies a ceiling, not a guaranteed tier. Item level must still meet the tier threshold, and weighted selection may choose any lower eligible tier. Common items have no explicit affix slots under the current pattern, but their guaranteed implicit can roll within the Common ceiling.

Tier weights are finite, positive, and nonincreasing as tier power rises. The initial shared balance template is:

|---|---:|

| T2 | 800 |

| T5 | 380 |

| T8 | 140 |
| T9 | 90 |
| T10 | 55 |
| T11 | 30 |
| T12 | 15 |

The accepted source manifest may tune stat-specific values within these eligibility and monotonicity rules. The final verification report records exact accepted weights and distribution results rather than treating this table as proof of balance.

## Weapon base damage

### Separate base property

Weapon damage is not an implicit, prefix, suffix, or `StatModifierSource`. It is an immutable base property of an issued damage-bearing weapon.

Support equipment does not receive a damage profile merely because its current `weight_class_id` or `weapon_family_id` contains weapon-like metadata. Shields, quivers, focuses, tomes, reliquaries, and similar items have no range unless a future design explicitly marks them as damage-bearing.

### Definition model

A damage-bearing `EquipmentBaseDefinition` references a typed `WeaponDamageProfile`. The profile contains:

- stable profile ID;
- optional minimum item level;
- one or more typed component curves;
- finite minimum and maximum anchor ranges over item levels 1 through 1000;
- rarity multipliers;
- an authored quality-roll policy;
- validation metadata used by reports and diagnostics.

Each component identifies a registered damage type. Most initial profiles contain one component:

- swords, daggers, bows, and ordinary hammers are primarily Physical;
- Emberweave may use Fire;
- Rime Scholar may use Cold;
- Storm Chaplain may use Lightning;
- Grave Covenant may use Chaos.

An intentionally hybrid weapon may contain two components. Duplicate damage types are invalid.

Future bases can use higher minimum item levels and stronger curves. Adding those bases does not change issued-item schema or recalculate existing weapons.

### Rarity scaling and permanent rolls

The item-level curve establishes the eligible range. Ordinary rarity multiplies that range:

| Rarity | Damage multiplier |
|---|---:|
| Common | 1.00 |
| Uncommon | 1.08 |
| Rare | 1.18 |
| Epic | 1.32 |
| Legendary | 1.50 |

A named deterministic `base_damage` substream rolls each component's quality without shifting base, rarity, pattern, affix, tier, or exact-affix-roll substreams. The result stores exact finite nonnegative minimum and maximum values with minimum less than or equal to maximum.

Balance-data changes affect future generation only.

## Item schema 2

`ItemInstance.SCHEMA_VERSION` advances from 1 to 2. Schema 2 adds an ordered `base_damage_components` array. Each component contains exactly:

- `damage_type_id`;
- `minimum_damage`;
- `maximum_damage`.

Non-damage-bearing items use an empty array. The array is sorted by stable damage-type ID before serialization. Values are finite JSON numbers and are copied defensively.

### Schema-1 compatibility

The decoder recognizes schema 1 explicitly and migrates it to an in-memory schema-2 item with an empty base-damage array. This preserves existing authored-action behavior and avoids inventing or rerolling historical weapon power.

The next successful profile save writes schema 2 atomically. A failed migration or save leaves the original profile bytes untouched. Schema-1 support has explicit tests and may not be removed merely because all developer fixtures have been regenerated.

Newly generated items and newly issued developer fixtures use schema 2.

## Deterministic generation flow

The generation sequence is:

1. Validate the typed request.
2. Select a base.
3. Select a rarity.
4. Resolve and roll typed base weapon damage.
5. Select a prefix/suffix pattern.
6. Assemble the guaranteed base implicit.
7. Select explicit affix families.
8. Select eligible tiers.
9. Roll exact affix values.
10. Issue one immutable schema-2 item.

The trace records a `base_damage` stage containing the profile, item-level inputs, rarity multiplier, component curves, deterministic quality values, and final ranges. A non-damage-bearing base records an explicit no-profile outcome rather than silently skipping explainability.

Generation remains pure with respect to profiles and ownership. A failure issues no item and mutates no sequence, inventory, stash, loadout, or profile state.

## Combat integration

### Weapon damage snapshot

The equipment activation transaction produces both:

- the existing validated equipment stat source; and
- a validated immutable active-weapon damage snapshot.

The snapshot includes member ID, item ID, base ID, typed ranges, and an equipment revision. It is not a stat modifier. Disabled or absent equipment contributes no weapon snapshot.

Both products publish atomically. Candidate stat resolution, weapon projection, and every affected owned action validate before the live member state changes. Any failure leaves the previous stat source, weapon snapshot, caches, health state, and signals unchanged.

### Action contract

An `AttackDefinition` explicitly chooses authored damage or weapon damage. Existing attacks default to authored damage.

A weapon-based action has a finite nonnegative weapon-effectiveness multiplier. When a valid active weapon snapshot exists, its typed base components are multiplied by effectiveness and then processed by the existing global, archetype, damage-type, critical, and cadence rules.

If no valid active weapon range exists, the action uses its authored damage components as fallback. This keeps unequipped characters functional and preserves schema-1 behavior. Caster spells and explicitly non-weapon abilities remain authored unless separately marked.

Runtime hits, action estimates, equipment comparisons, and ledger rows consume the same projection service. No second damage formula is permitted.

The initial action audit marks only clearly weapon-based playable actions. Ambiguous magical or conversion actions remain authored until a later conversion/added-damage design can express them without hidden behavior.

## Presentation

Item tooltips show information in this order:

1. base name, rarity, and item level;
2. typed base weapon ranges, when present;
3. guaranteed implicit;
4. explicit prefixes and suffixes;
5. requirements and disabled-state explanation;
6. advanced and developer details.

Multi-component weapons show each damage type separately using the damage-type presentation color and accessible text. Advanced details show the base profile, rarity multiplier, damage quality, affix names, tiers, exact values, and tier roll bounds.

Comparison rows use the shared green/improved and red/reduced direction contract for:

- each base damage component;
- total normal hit;
- critical hit;
- average hit;
- attacks per second;
- estimated DPS;
- healing metrics where applicable;
- equipment activation or disabled-state changes.

Armoury, Warehouse, and Developer Sandbox receive this presentation. The Character Ledger Equipment & Inventory tab remains `Coming Soon` until its separate implementation increment.

## Validation and diagnostics

Validation rejects:

- production manifest totals other than 96 explicit and 99 implicit definitions;
- prefix/suffix or focused/hybrid/premium count drift;
- any production explicit or implicit without exactly twelve tiers;
- duplicate IDs, missing base assignments, duplicate base implicits, unknown references, or unreachable definitions;
- invalid weights, nonmonotonic thresholds or values, inverted roll ranges, or invalid rarity ceilings;
- unknown, empty, or duplicate modifier families and tags;
- hybrid family declarations that allow equivalent focused effects to stack unintentionally;
- missing or malformed weapon profiles;
- unknown or duplicate damage types;
- nonfinite, negative, inverted, or nonmonotonic weapon curves and issued ranges;
- a support base receiving a damage range without an explicit damage profile;
- malformed schema-2 documents or unsupported future schema versions;
- weapon-based actions with invalid effectiveness or no authored fallback;
- runtime and estimate projections that disagree or become nonfinite.

Stable errors use the existing prefixes and add precise stages where needed, including:

- `PARTY_FORGE_ITEM_GENERATION_ERROR stage=base_damage ...`;
- `PARTY_FORGE_ITEM_ERROR field=base_damage_components[...] ...`;
- `PARTY_FORGE_EQUIPMENT_PROJECTION_ERROR ...`;
- `PARTY_FORGE_DAMAGE_ERROR attack=... ...`.

No validator repairs, substitutes, rerolls, or partially applies invalid content.

## Balance reports

Increment 3 produces deterministic machine-readable and human-readable reports covering:

- exact manifest counts and category totals;
- affix and tier reachability;
- prefix/suffix fill rates by rarity pattern;
- selection frequency for all four weight bands;
- item-level samples at every approved threshold and at 1000;
- Common-through-Legendary tier distributions;
- melee/ranged/caster and item-slot coverage;
- accessory breadth and off-family reachability;
- party-bias behavior without elimination of global/off-party bases;
- diminishing Charisma effects without gate bypass;
- implicit coverage for all 99 bases;
- weapon damage by base, item level, rarity, component type, and quality percentile;
- regression seeds for minimum, median, high, and edge-case outcomes.

These reports are development evidence, not final economy or mode-drop balance. Increment 4 exposes suitable analysis through the Developer Loot Lab.

## Verification strategy

### Unit and catalog coverage

- Exact 96/99/48/48/64/24/8 content counts.
- Exactly twelve tiers per production definition.
- Every base has exactly one registered implicit.
- Every damage-bearing base has one valid profile; every support base has none.
- Eligibility, family exclusion, rarity ceilings, item-level thresholds, weights, and roll bounds.
- Cold byte-identical builder parity.
- Schema-1 migration and schema-2 encode/decode/copy/validation.
- Named-substream determinism and independence.
- Weapon profile interpolation, rarity scaling, quality rolling, and typed-component ordering.

### Runtime and transactional coverage

- Active equipment publishes stat and weapon products atomically.
- Disabled, removed, replaced, restored, and incompatible weapons update both products correctly.
- Failed equipment changes publish nothing and emit no success signals.
- Weapon-based actions use weapon ranges; authored actions ignore them.
- Missing weapon data uses authored fallback.
- Runtime damage and action estimates match for physical, elemental, chaos, and hybrid ranges.
- Equipment comparisons color direction correctly and include damage/DPS effects.
- Save/load, extraction, Armoury, Warehouse, Developer Sandbox, and profile ownership remain intact.

### Scale and integration coverage

- Deterministic distribution samples cover representative seeds, item levels, rarities, Heat, Charisma, and party compositions.
- All 99 bases and all 195 production affix/implicit definitions are reachable where authorized.
- Twenty-four-member isolation proves one member's loadout and weapon snapshot cannot change another member.
- Cold import, focused suites, complete suite, startup readiness, and save compatibility pass at the exact review commit.
- Generated sidecars and task artifacts are inventoried and handled without deleting unrelated user files.

Physical-controller acceptance and human visual review are recorded as deferred unless actually performed. Headless tests do not satisfy either gate.

## Acceptance criteria

Increment 3 is complete when:

1. The catalog registers exactly 96 production explicit affixes and 99 production implicits with all approved category totals.
2. Every production explicit and implicit has twelve valid tiers and deterministic byte-identical generated data.
3. All 99 active bases have one distinct guaranteed implicit.
4. Eligibility and weights produce broad accessories, constrained combat gear, reachable off-party outcomes, and scarce premium hybrids.
5. Progressive rarity ceilings and item-level thresholds operate without guaranteeing high tiers.
6. Damage-bearing weapons generate immutable typed ranges using the approved rarity multipliers.
7. Schema-1 items remain loadable without rerolling, while new items round-trip as schema 2.
8. Weapon-aware actions, runtime damage, ledgers, and comparisons share one authoritative projection.
9. Equipment transitions remain atomic and disabled items contribute neither affixes nor weapon damage.
10. Deterministic balance reports cover the approved content, level, rarity, archetype, party-bias, Charisma, and weapon matrices.
11. Existing ownership, extraction, storage, profile, responsive, and controller contracts remain intact.
12. Focused, integration, complete-suite, startup, cold-import, and save-compatibility gates pass with exact evidence.

## Future seams

Later increments may add additional bases, higher base-power curves, more damage types, conversion, added weapon damage, dual-wield selection rules, conditional affixes, Legendary powers, upper-rarity acquisition, crafting weights, passive-tree modifiers, and ground-drop presentation.

Those systems must extend the typed base-damage, item-instance, affix-family, ownership, and stat-resolution contracts. They may not reroll historical items, bypass equipment transactions, or introduce competing item or damage identities.
